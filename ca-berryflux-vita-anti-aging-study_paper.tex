\begin{abstract}
Background: This study evaluates the efficacy, texture performance, and consumer conversion metrics of RIMAN Skincare's proprietary active, Berryflux Vita (derived from raspberry plant cell culture), within the diverse climatic zones of Canada. Methods: A stratified survey of N=200 participants across Montreal, Toronto, and Vancouver assessed Likert-scale perceptions of moisture retention, elasticity improvement, and price sensitivity in CAD, alongside Net Promoter Score (NPS) analysis. Results: The overall composite mean was 3.49 with a Top-2-Box percentage of 48.7\%. Notably, texture absorption in low-humidity environments scored high (Mean 3.88, Top-2-Box 85.5\%), while perceived elasticity gains after two weeks remained moderate (Mean 3.28). Winter harsh cold triggered the highest willingness to pay (98.0\%). Strategic Implications: Despite strong starter-to-full-ritual conversion rates (57.5\%), the negative NPS (-24.5) highlights a critical gap between initial trial satisfaction and long-term advocacy, driven primarily by uncertainty regarding efficacy claims (95.5\% barrier rate). This report provides actionable insights for optimizing RIMAN Mall's regional strategy and enhancing trust in INCELLDERM technology.

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} RIMAN Skincare, INCELLDERM, Berryflux Vita, Microclimate Adaptation, Consumer Conversion, Canadian Dermatology
\end{abstract}

\section{1. Formulation Architecture \& Study Methodology}
The core of this investigation centers on Berryflux Vita, a patented active ingredient derived from raspberry plant cell culture, integrated into RIMAN Skincare's formulation architecture. The study employed a rigorous stratified sampling method involving 200 Canadian consumers distributed across three distinct microclimates: Montreal (Subarctic Winter, 39\% RH), Toronto (Cold Continental, 42\% RH), and Vancouver (Pacific Maritime, 72\% RH). Participants were evaluated using a 5-point Likert scale for key dermatological benefits including moisture retention, skin elasticity, and antioxidant support, as well as single-choice questions regarding competitive benchmarking and seasonal purchasing triggers. The methodology specifically targeted the 'Descending INCI' principle, ensuring that the concentration and origin of the active ingredient were transparently communicated to assess its impact on perceived safety and purity.
\section{2. Quantitative Performance \& Microclimate Adaptation}
Quantitative data reveals significant variance in product performance based on environmental conditions. The overall composite mean stood at 3.49, with a Top-2-Box score of 48.7\%. In terms of specific attributes, the texture of products containing the raspberry plant cell culture active demonstrated exceptional performance in low-humidity environments, achieving a mean score of 3.88 and an 85.5\% Top-2-Box rating for quick absorption without heavy residue. Conversely, moisture retention adequacy for dry, cold winters received a more neutral response (Mean 3.50, Top-2-Box 49.5\%). Seasonal analysis indicated that winter harsh cold is the primary driver for premium moisturizing purchases, with 98.0\% of respondents citing it as their highest willingness-to-pay trigger. However, perceived tangible improvement in skin elasticity after a two-week period using the Purcell Cleansing Oil variant remained modest (Mean 3.28, Top-2-Box 28.5\%), suggesting a need for longer observation periods or enhanced delivery systems.
\section{3. Cross-Demographic, MBTI \& B2B/C-End Stratification}
Demographic stratification highlights distinct behavioral patterns. Affluent/High Net Worth individuals exhibited higher satisfaction scores (Mean 3.72) compared to Lower-Middle Income groups (Mean 3.28). Professionally, B2B stakeholders such as Aesthetic Clinic Owners reported the highest composite means (3.72), indicating strong professional endorsement, whereas C-End users like Software Engineers showed lower engagement (3.43). Psychographic analysis via MBTI revealed that ESTJ personalities had the highest mean score (3.61), valuing structured efficacy, while ISFPs recorded the lowest (3.32), potentially due to aesthetic or sensory mismatches. Skin type segmentation showed Mature/Photo-Aged users responding most positively (Mean 3.60), aligning with the product's anti-aging focus. Crucially, the transition from trial to full-size purchase saw a 57.5\% conversion rate, driven primarily by 'Brand Patent Trust' (87.5\%) and 'Cost Per Use Value' (87.5\%). However, 'Uncertain Efficacy' remains the dominant barrier (95.5\%), particularly among those waiting 5-8 weeks for visible results.
\section{4. Regulatory Compliance \& Regional Expansion Strategy}
Strategic implications for RIMAN Mall and regional expansion are deeply tied to regulatory perception and competitive positioning. 76.5\% of respondents benchmarked Berryflux Vita against Local Canadian Derm Brands, signaling a direct competition with established domestic players rather than global luxury brands. Confidence in regulatory compliance ensuring long-term availability was rated moderately high (Mean 3.51, Top-2-Box 52.0\%). However, the Net Promoter Score (NPS) presents a critical challenge, registering at -24.5 with only 3.0\% promoters versus 27.5\% detractors. This negative sentiment correlates strongly with the 'Lack of Local Availability' barrier (70.5\%) and 'Ingredient Confusion' (47.0\%). To improve ROI, RIMAN must enhance educational content regarding the 'Antioxidant Support' patent, which currently has low justification for switching routines (Top-2-Box 21.0\%). Strengthening local distribution networks and clarifying the scientific basis of the raspberry plant cell culture origin will be pivotal in converting passive users into advocates.

\section{Conclusion}
This study confirms that while RIMAN Skincare's Berryflux Vita excels in texture performance and seasonal relevance for Canadian winters, it faces significant hurdles in efficacy perception and net promoter advocacy. The high conversion rate from starter kits indicates strong initial interest, but the substantial barrier of 'Uncertain Efficacy' and negative NPS suggest that current communication strategies fail to adequately substantiate the 'Moisture' and 'Elasticity' patents. Future success depends on bridging the gap between clinical promise and consumer experience through localized education, improved supply chain visibility, and reinforced trust in the proprietary INCELLDERM technology.
